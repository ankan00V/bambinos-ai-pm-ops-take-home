A · Executive summary

The pipeline leaks in three places, and none of them is effort

The pipeline is not slow because Ops is slow. In all four cases where a form arrived and Ops approved it, the approval and the booking request were created in the same minute. The delay sits upstream in a form that repeats information the payment already holds, and downstream in an allocation step that has no named owner.

On this data, 2 of 12 paid invoices never got a form at all. Another 8 of 12 forms were still unapproved at the snapshot, the oldest for 32.5 hours. Two of the four bookings never produced a session. The end state is 11 paying students with no future class in the schedule export, worth INR 126,850 of the INR 138,850 collected.

The fix is to stop treating the teacher form as the trigger. For a same-batch renewal the invoice already carries the student, the class, the batch, the teacher, the amount and the discount. The only field the form adds is a preferred time, and in 11 of 12 forms that time is identical to the batch the child is already in. The form is not a control, it is a queue.

I built the engine that acts on that: 700 lines of Python, no runtime dependencies, 32 tests covering every reason code, an n8n export and a Google Sheets spec that mirror the logic, and three prompt files. It auto-approves 7 of 13 cases today and would clear 9 of 12 paid renewals with no human. The five blocking exceptions it leaves have an owner, an SLA and a drafted message.

That is worth about 144 ops hours a month at 1,000 renewals, INR 43,186, against a one-time build of about INR 2,00,000. The ask is small: three weeks in shadow mode, and one live-roster validation that confirms the same-batch renewal retains an existing seat.

Five files, 44 rows. I read every row before writing a rule, because a rule built on an assumed schema is how you get a matcher that scores the wrong invoice at 0.97.

Joins, ranked by how much I trust them. student_id is exact in 13 of 13 invoices and 12 of 12 forms. invoice_id is exact in 10 of 12 forms; one is a typo (84931 against a real 84913) and one is a duplicate submission. faculty_form_id links only 4 of 12 forms to a booking. bookingrequest_id reaches only one of four bookings. The invoice link is well formed in all 12 forms but must never be used as a key, because two invoice URLs differ by three characters out of fifty.

form_submission_lag is measurable for 10 forms, approval_lag for 4, and payment to schedule for exactly 1. I report the sample size next to every timing number, because a median of one is an anecdote.

What this data cannot settle. All names, phones and emails are masked, so fuzzy name matching cannot be validated here. There are no term start or end dates, so class interruption is a proxy: money received and no future session row. There is no gateway settlement feed, so a late payment or a chargeback is invisible. And the schedule table has 2 rows for 12 paid renewals, which is either a catastrophe or a partial export. I treat it as partial and say so wherever the number appears.

Three days of data, 13 cases, one Sunday to Tuesday window. Every monthly figure in this report is a scenario with the volume assumption written next to it, not a measurement.

A · Leakage audit

Where students get stuck, stage by stage

Thirteen invoices were raised. Twelve collected money. Eleven distinct invoices got a form. Four were approved. Four bookings were created. One student has session rows.

Each drop has a different cause, which matters because no single fix would have caught all of them. The two orphans needed no form at all: one is a GCC parent who paid INR 18,000, the other a chess parent who paid INR 11,000, and neither teacher submitted anything. The eight unapproved forms needed a decision that never came. The three unscheduled bookings exposed two allocation failures and one false batch-full rule.

The shape of the funnel is the argument for the redesign. The middle of the pipeline, where a human retypes a payment into a form and another human checks it, holds no information the system did not already have. It only adds waiting, and two ways to lose a child: the form never arrives, or the approval never happens.

Five things the brief did not ask for

The batch-full block is a counting artefact. Batch 9205 is at 6 of 6 and student 68105 is one of those six. A same-batch renewal extends that occupied seat rather than taking a seventh, so the engine retains it automatically while keeping a hard capacity block for a genuinely new allocation.

Approval is not the bottleneck. All four approved forms produced a booking in the same minute. Then booking 5502 sat at Pending_Batch_Allocation for 51.7 hours. A faster approval inbox would move the wrong number.

A discount looks like an underpayment. Form 3307 claims 7,600 against a 9,500 list price. The invoice records a 1,900 discount, exactly 20%, so the claim matches the net amount. Any rule that compares the form to the gross price flags every scholarship renewal.

The form is not independent evidence. On 84904 the invoice carries a number that appears nowhere else, and the form supplies the one that matches the parent record. The teacher typed it from the same WhatsApp thread that produced the payment, so it is a copy of the conversation, not a second source.

Cross-border money is unreconciled. Two Stripe payments, from a UAE and a US number, both billed in INR for INR 40,000 combined. There is no currency field in the reference table, so nothing in this data can confirm the settlement amount.

B · Process audit

Why each manual step exists, and which one to delete

The brief asks whether the teacher form can be eliminated for clean renewals. The answer is arithmetic, not opinion. Take the four fields the form exists to capture and ask where else they live.

StepStated purposeDoes it holdDecision
Invoice created by the gatewayRecord that the parent paidHolds. It is the only source of truth for money.Keep, and promote to the trigger for the whole flow.
Teacher fills a renewal formCapture student, batch, amount, preferred timeFour of five fields are already on the invoice. The fifth matches the current batch in 11 of 12 forms.Remove as a trigger. Keep as corroboration and as the carrier of a batch-change request.
Ops approves the formVerify the payment before bookingVerification is three field comparisons. Approval and booking creation landed in the same minute in all four cases.Replace with rules. Humans keep capacity, identity and policy decisions.
Batch allocated, sessions createdParent or Ops picks days and timeFor a same-batch renewal there is nothing to pick. Booking 5502 waited 51.7 hours for a click.Auto-allocate same-batch. Escalate only batch changes.

The only field the form genuinely adds is the preferred day and time. In 11 of 12 forms it is identical to the batch the student is already in, because the parent is renewing the same slot with the same teacher. The twelfth is a real batch change, and that is the one case where a form is still worth collecting.

So the redesign does not delete the form. It demotes it. The invoice triggers the flow, the form becomes a piece of evidence that can block an edge case but never gates a clean one, and the queue that produced 27 minutes of median waiting and a 16.7% orphan rate stops existing.

B · Re-engineered workflow

One trigger, two lanes, no form in the critical path

The fast lane is deliberately narrow. All four conditions must hold: the invoice is Paid or Partially_Paid with the net equal to billed minus discount; the student, at least one contact channel, the teacher and the class match the active record; and the renewal is same-batch and the form, when present, names that batch. That continuation retains its existing seat. Any new allocation must reserve capacity; if it cannot, the case goes to the exception lane. It never half-approves.

In the exception lane the case keeps everything the fast lane would have given it: the resolved invoice, the batch, the amount, the payer, and a sentence explaining exactly which check failed. What changes is that a named person now owns it, with a clock running.

C · Prototype

Deterministic rules, with the LLM kept on a short leash

Five CSVs in, six operational artifacts out, one command. The pipeline is load, normalize, match, decide, allocate seats, write. No runtime dependencies, no network calls, no wall clock in the outputs.

The rule order, which is also the blast radius

  1. Resolve the invoice: exact id, then link, then fuzzy repair with a uniqueness requirement.
  2. Payment settled: status must be Paid or Partially_Paid.
  3. Fee settled: net must equal billed minus discount, within tolerance.
  4. Claim matches: the form amount must equal the net collected.
  5. Identity: student id, then at least one contact channel.
  6. Batch: same-batch, active, correct teacher and class, valid session count.
  7. Seats: same-batch renewals retain an existing seat; new allocations reserve one through a ledger.
  8. State: already scheduled, approved but unscheduled, or approved with no booking.
ParameterValueWhy that value
Invoice similarity floor0.7284931 to 84913 scores 0.80; unrelated numbers score near 0
Runner-up margin0.05refuse when two candidates are close, rather than guessing
Amount toleranceINR 1 or 0.5%gateway rounding, nothing more
Stuck invoice threshold12 hoursthe dashboard tile the ops head asked for
Escalation clock24 hoursone business day

What it refuses to do. It will not approve against a failed payment, even with a screenshot. It will not pick between two plausible invoices; it returns R02 and asks a teacher for the number. It will not edit an invoice amount to match a form. And it will not put a second booking on an invoice that already has one.

Every row carries a reason code and a sentence with the numbers in it. That is the difference between a script and something an ops team can trust at 9am on a Monday.

Where the LLM sits. Three prompts in prompts/. One polishes message copy, one resolves genuinely ambiguous invoice references, one triages an exception that the rule set has not seen before. All three take a facts payload and are forbidden from adding a number, a date or a promise. The default run does not call any of them, and the output is identical without a key.

That is a deliberate cost decision as much as a safety one. Message drafting is the only place where wording quality matters and a wrong answer is cheap. Decisions are not.

Today the same 1,000 renewals cost 162.7 ops hours: 7.81 minutes of task time per case, inflated by a 25% context-switch allowance. The redesign lands at 18.7 hours, because 70% of cases need a quarter of a minute of oversight and the remaining 30% need 3.1 minutes each.

What is not in the number. Recovered revenue. If faster scheduling stopped even 2% of at-risk renewals from lapsing, that would add roughly INR 2.8 lakh a month at this average net, which dwarfs the labour saving. I left it out because the churn rate is invented and the labour saving is not.

Every input sits in engine/savings_assumptions.json. Change the volume to 300 and the hours scale linearly; the payback moves to 14 months. Nothing in the model is hidden.
D · Rollout

SOPs, 30-60-90, and how a rollback cannot break a class

Teachers. The SOP is five things they may be asked, four rules that save them time, and a list of what we will never ask them to do, including chase a parent for money. The headline for them is that the form is gone for same-batch renewals, so this rollout removes admin rather than adding it. That is the whole adoption argument.

Ops. The SOP replaces the approval inbox with an exception queue. Work oldest first, close on state change rather than on reply, and never edit an invoice amount to match a form. The weekly review is 30 minutes on cases that took more than 24 hours, because three weeks of that list tells you which rule to change next.

WindowFocusGate to pass
Days 0 to 30Shadow mode. Engine recommends, Ops decides. Every disagreement logged with one line of why.Zero disagreements on the last 50 cases of a code before it can go live.
Days 31 to 60Pilot with two teachers and about 20% of volume. Only R00 and R17 write.No wrong child scheduled, auto-approval rate within 10 points of prediction.

Keeping live schedules safe

The engine never edits an existing session row. It writes bookings through one endpoint, and only for invoices with no existing booking. Before any write it produces the same rows in shadow mode so the diff can be read. Rollback is one switch that returns writes to manual while the engine keeps recommending, so a rollback cannot orphan a class that already exists.

Rollback criteria, written before launch. More than 0.5% false approvals in the audit sample. Any case where the wrong child is scheduled. More than two escalations a day caused by the engine rather than by the parent. Any breach of the 12 hour stuck threshold that the engine created rather than inherited.

The pilot is two teachers, not two hundred, because the failure modes I care about are social: a teacher who keeps filling forms out of habit, an ops agent who overrides without a reason. Both show up in week one with two teachers and are invisible at scale.

What I would do differently

I would start from the schedule table, not the invoices. The funnel looks like an approval problem until you notice that approval and booking creation happen in the same minute, and the real gap is allocation. Reading the last table first would have found that in ten minutes instead of an hour.

I would also instrument time-in-state from day one. Every number in this report is reconstructed from timestamps that happen to exist, and two of the eight metrics have a sample size small enough that I have to apologise for them. A single state_changed_at column would make all eight measurable.

And I would ask for one unmasked sample of 50 rows. The masked data is honest about structure and useless for validating the one thing that needs it, which is whether two names that look alike belong to the same child.

What I am not claiming

This is a 13 case sample, not a production baseline. The interruption rate rests on a two row schedule table. The LLM path has never run against a real key. The n8n export is structurally valid and its JavaScript passes a syntax check, but it has not been imported into a live instance. The Sheets spec has not been pasted into a sheet.

What I am claiming is narrower and, I think, more useful: on the data provided, three quarters of paid renewals need no human, the five that do have a name and a clock, and every decision the machine makes can be read and argued with by the person who owns the class.
